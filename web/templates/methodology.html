Methodology

This page summarizes how every calculation works. The complete version, including all formulas and known limitations, is METHODOLOGY.md in the source repository.

1. Bill reconstruction

A standardized monthly bill is built from separate energy, generation, transmission, distribution, regulatory, tax, and subsidy components — never one blended "electricity rate."

2-3. Household consumption and hourly readiness

Household demand is baseline consumption plus optional EV, heat pump, water heater, cooling, and other additions, minus efficiency savings. The public interface shows monthly results; the underlying data model is hourly-ready for future phases, but Phase 1's monthly aggregation is a labelled approximation, not a validated hourly simulation.

4. Solar production

Monthly production scales with system size, a location-specific monthly yield, and (1 − degradation)^age.

5. Net-metering credit ledger

Exported solar becomes a non-cash credit tracked in dated tranches that expire 12 months after creation, applied only to the eligible electricity energy charge — never to fixed delivery, ineligible variable delivery, regulatory charges, or tax.

6. Purchase cost and financing

Group-purchase discounts apply only to eligible quote components. Standard loan amortization computes payments and interest; interest avoided by a discount is reported separately from the discount's direct price reduction.

7-8. Price and cost-decline scenarios

Each bill component and the installed solar cost have their own separate real annual growth/decline assumption, distinct from general inflation and from each other, recorded in versioned scenario files that are never edited in place once published.

9. Provincial demand (context only)

IESO provincial system demand-growth scenarios are shown as context for why prices are uncertain — never converted into a household kWh addition or a retail-price growth number.

10. Twenty-year comparison

For each purchase-timing option, nominal and discounted (NPV) totals are computed across grid bills, solar cost, financing, maintenance, replacement, incentives, and eligible unexpired credits.

11. Cost of waiting

Solves for the minimum annual real solar-cost decline required for waiting to beat buying now, under the selected assumptions, and states the result alongside those assumptions.

12. Sensitivity analysis

Recomputes total cost while independently varying each major assumption, ranked by effect size.

13. Battery and generator boundaries

Validated hourly battery-dispatch and generator fuel-cost modelling is deferred to Phase 3. Resilience value is reported separately from ordinary energy economics and never assigned an invented dollar value by default.

14. Known limitations

  • Household and solar hourly shapes are documented representative estimates, not measured for any individual home.
  • Only Hydro Ottawa is currently modelled.
  • Ultra-low overnight pricing is defined but not yet exposed.
  • Solar-resource data uses a single Ottawa-region profile.
  • Illustrative scenarios are modelling assumptions, not official forecasts.
  • Battery and generator dispatch modelling is deferred to Phase 3.
  • Net-metering eligible-charge rules should be re-verified against current OEB and distributor billing practice before relying on them for a real decision.
